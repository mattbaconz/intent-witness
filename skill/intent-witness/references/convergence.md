# Convention vs Unjustified Convergence

## Never use frequency as proof of bad design

A pattern being common is only a reason to investigate, not a reason to remove it.

## Convention is likely justified when

- users already understand the pattern and it shortens task time;
- the information naturally fits the representation;
- the pattern clearly expresses hierarchy or comparison;
- product research/explicit intent supports it;
- alternative structures would add novelty but not value.

## Convergence is suspicious when

- a structure appears despite weak mapping to the primary user job;
- multiple semantically different objects receive identical treatment;
- the first viewport resembles a generic category template while product-specific state is secondary;
- section/route labels are generic and overlapping;
- styling is changed repeatedly but macrostructure never changes;
- references are copied as aesthetic recipes rather than principles;
- mobile simply preserves desktop component order.

## Counterexamples

### KPI cards can be correct
An executive monitoring surface may genuinely begin with health KPIs because the primary job is scanning status.

### Cards can be correct
A marketplace of independent items may need clear repeated containers with discrete actions.

### Gradient hero can be correct
A creative product may use a highly expressive hero that directly demonstrates generative capability.

### Generic settings patterns can be correct
Commodity preferences benefit from familiar toggles/sections rather than invented interaction.

The reviewer should reward **fit**, not novelty.
