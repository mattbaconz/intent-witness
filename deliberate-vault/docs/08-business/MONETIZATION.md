# Monetization

## Business risk

Developer tools can achieve large GitHub mindshare while generating little revenue. “Robot tells me my UI is generic” may not justify a personal subscription.

Monetization should focus on **operational/team value**, not access to basic taste rules.

## Potential tiers (conceptual)

### OSS / Free

- local skill and reviewer;
- local evidence engine;
- project design memory;
- basic adapters;
- public benchmarks.

### Pro / Individual (only if demand exists)

Possible value:

- managed model routing;
- hosted review artifact history;
- higher-cost deep multimodal reviews;
- private cross-project design profiles;
- convenience integrations.

This tier may be weak; do not assume it is necessary.

### Team

Stronger value:

- shared design language;
- PR review integration;
- review policies;
- organization history;
- private eval fixtures;
- role/access control;
- Figma/design-system sync;
- centralized cost/privacy configuration.

### Enterprise

Potential:

- self-hosting/private model options;
- SSO/audit logs;
- retention controls;
- policy management;
- dedicated integrations/support.

## Pricing principle

Price against saved review time/design consistency, not token counts or number of “slop detections.”

Avoid per-finding pricing; it incentivizes noisy critique.

## Revenue validation questions

- Would a team pay to have every agent-written frontend PR reviewed?
- Is design review a real bottleneck or just aesthetic annoyance?
- Does Deliberate catch costly regressions early enough to justify budget?
- Who owns budget: engineering, design, or developer productivity?
- Is the product a standalone category or a feature inside CI/ADE platforms?

## Strategic option: remain FOSS

A successful open-source project can be valuable even without SaaS: reputation, ecosystem leverage, sponsorship, consulting, hiring/partnership opportunities, or future adjacent products.

Do not force monetization if it compromises adoption or focus.
